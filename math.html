The Mathematics of Password Entropy

Understanding why length mathematically dominates complexity, how attackers model human predictability, and why slow cryptographic hashing protects breached databases.

1. Calculating Bit Entropy (H) Information Theory

In cryptography, entropy measures the unpredictability of a credential in bits. Every additional bit of entropy doubles the number of guesses an attacker must make on average.

H = L × log2(N)

3. Why Storage Hashing Matters (MD5 vs. Argon2id)

Even a strong password can be compromised if a server stores it using a fast cryptographic checksum like MD5 or SHA-1 instead of a memory-hard key derivation function:

  • ⚠️
    Unsalted MD5 / SHA-1 (Broken for Passwords): Designed for speed. A modern multi-GPU rig can compute over 100 billion hashes per second and look up precomputed Rainbow Tables instantly.
  • 🛡️
    bcrypt / PBKDF2 (Industry Standard): Introduces a unique cryptographic salt per user (defeating Rainbow Tables) and a configurable work factor that deliberately slows down brute-force attempts.
  • 🔒
    Argon2id (OWASP Top Recommendation): Memory-hard algorithm that requires dedicated RAM per guess, neutralizing parallel GPU and ASIC cracking hardware.
